Problem Definition: Indian Railways generates massive volumes of operational data daily -- scheduled timetables, station master records, and actual arrival/departure logs. Train delays across extensive rail networks disrupt travel schedules, reduce operational efficiency, and cascade into system-wide congestion. This project aims to analyze historical train delay data to uncover underlying punctuality patterns, pinpoint high-risk stations and bottlenecks, and forecast delays to enable proactive operational and commuter planning.

Project Description: This project focuses on evaluating a comprehensive dataset of passenger train operations across the Indian Railways network over a full year. By analyzing scheduled timetables, station sequences, and recorded delays across multiple service types, the project addresses three primary objectives:
- Punctuality & Temporal Pattern Analysis: Characterizing delay distributions across different times of the year, days of the week, and train categories (e.g., Express, Superfast, Premium services).
- Bottleneck & High-Risk Route Identification: Mapping geographical and structural stress points across railway zones, identifying critical transit stations and sections that consistently trigger or amplify delays.
- Delay Prediction: Forecasting anticipated arrival and departure delays at subsequent stations along a route, transforming raw operational records into actionable foresight.

DATASET DETAILS:
Source: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/rxydenxd/indian-railways-delay-dataset

This dataset contains the delay information for about 7,000 trains from Indian railways network for the past one year (2025-02-08 to 2026-02-07), for each day, for each station in their route.

This dataset is scraped entirely from scratch (etrain.info) using Python by the user with username "RXYDENXD" at www.kaggle.com. The goal was to build a comprehensive, multi-table dataset covering the schedules, station metadata, and real-world delay records for nearly the entire Indian Railways passenger network, spanning a full year of operations.

The scraping pipeline used requests, pandas, multi-threaded scraping, which reduced collection time by approximately 90% compared to sequential scraping.

------------------------------------------------------
*   Technique Used: Application of a Global Constant for Nominal Attributes.
*   Before: The resolved station dimension (`dim_station_resolved.csv`) contained 94 missing `station_address` values.

2. Schedule Fact Cleaning: Missing Arrival & Departure Times
------------------------------------------------------------
*   Technique Used: Logical Imputation (Zero-Halt Rule) for Interval-Scaled Attributes.
*   Before: The `combined_schedule.csv` dataset had 8,673 missing `arrival_time` and 8,673 missing `departure_time` values. A verification script proved these missing states were structural and intentional: missing arrivals occurred exactly and only at origin stations (`station_no = 1`), while missing departures occurred exactly and only at destination stations.
*   Resolution Applied: The Zero-Halt imputation rule was applied using Pandas. Missing origin arrival times were filled with their scheduled departure time, and missing destination departure times were filled with their scheduled arrival time.
*   After/Result: 0 remaining missing schedule times. This resolves the missing data while preserving the continuous numeric sequences required for time-series predictive models. The cleaned dataset was saved as `results/combined_schedule_imputed.csv`.

3. Delay Field Context Resolution (important for handling missing delay values)
---------------------------------
*   Technique Used: Contextual Hypothesis Testing via Out-of-Core Processing.
*   Before: The exact operational definition of the `delay` column in the ~30 million row `combined_delay.csv` dataset was ambiguous (whether it represented arrival delay or departure delay).
    Our defined composite hypothesis posits that the delay field is a context-dependent metric that switches meaning based on the station's position in the route. Specifically, it assumes that:
        - Departure Delay is recorded exclusively at the train's source or origin station.
        - Arrival Delay is recorded at all subsequent intermediate and destination stations.

*   Resolution Applied: A hypothesis was formulated to test delays at route boundaries (origin vs. destination). A DuckDB SQL query, explicitly filtering out null values, evaluated all 38 million records and yielded 1,038,047 non-zero delays at origin stations and 1,044,006 non-zero delays at destination stations.
*   After/Result: The composite context switch hypothesis was confirmed. The `delay` field is context-dependent: it strictly records Departure Delay at the source station (`station_no = 1`) and Arrival Delay at all intermediate and destination stations (`station_no > 1`). These findings establish the foundational ETL rules for numeric modeling and were successfully exported to `results/step02_01_ETL_FINDINGS.json`.

ETL / DATA CLEANING (STEP 2.2) — SPATIOTEMPORAL IMPUTATION OF MISSING DELAYS

Following the resolution of the delay field context, the ETL pipeline addressed the 1,874,271 structured missing values in the 38-million-row combined_delay.csv fact table. Because train delays cascade sequentially across a network, missing values could not be blindly replaced with zero. A three-step out-of-core DuckDB script was executed to resolve these gaps while preserving time-series continuity for future predictive models.

1. Cancellation Drop Rule

Initial Discovery: Certain train journeys logged zero valid delays across their entire route, representing completely missing sequences (cancelled or short-terminated trains).

Technique Applied: Group-wise filtering and conditional deletion.

Resolution: Journeys with 0 valid delays were identified and entirely dropped before any imputation occurred. This prevented the pipeline from generating "phantom trains" that would artificially skew bottleneck identification.

2. Origin Imputation

Initial Discovery: Forward-filling cannot apply to origin stations (station_no = 1) because there is no preceding station to pull data from.

Technique Applied: Class-specific central tendency.

Resolution: Missing departure delays at origin stations were replaced with the median departure delay calculated exclusively for that specific train_no. This established a mathematically sound baseline for the start of the journey.

3. Spatiotemporal Imputation (Forward-Filling)

Initial Discovery: Intermediate and destination stations contained mid-route reporting gaps due to tracking blackouts.

Technique Applied: Spatiotemporal Interpolation (Last Observation Carried Forward).

Resolution: A time-series window function (LAST_VALUE ... IGNORE NULLS) partitioned by date and train_no was used to drag the preceding known physical delay forward to fill the intermediate gaps. This bridged temporary reporting gaps without breaking the continuous numeric sequence required for time-shifted delta calculations.

4. Verification and Export

Technique Applied: Statistical auditing using Pandas and DuckDB.

Result: The datasets successfully verified at 0 remaining missing values. The fully imputed 38-million-row (roughly) fact table was safely exported to results/combined_delay_imputed.csv without modifying the raw source data.

ETL / DATA CLEANING (STEP 2.3) — NOISE REDUCTION AND OUTLIER BOUNDARY DEFINITION

Following the spatiotemporal imputation of missing delays, the final step of the ETL pipeline focused on smoothing noisy continuous variables and defining the mathematical boundaries for extreme real-world outliers (bottlenecks) in the 38-million-row delay dataset. 

Defining Noise vs. Outliers in Train Delays:
* Noise: (E.g., faulty instruments, data entry problems, transmission errors). For example, a typographical error logging a 1,000,000-minute delay is garbage data that must be smoothed or removed before outlier detection to prevent it from blurring the distinction between normal objects and true anomalies.
* Outliers: A data object that deviates significantly from the rest of the dataset, appearing as if it were generated by a different mechanism.
* The Project Context: In anomaly mining, "one person’s garbage could be another person’s treasure". While a 14-hour delay deviates wildly from the mean, it is likely an exceptional treasure indicating a severe structural bottleneck rather than a random error. Our goal is to preserve these genuine extremes while smoothing out the impossible transmission errors.

1. Negative Delay (Early Arrival) Validation & Punctuality
* Initial Discovery: A preliminary DuckDB query identified 850,395 negative delay records.
* Domain Verification: Since the dataset was scraped from an Indian Railways tracking platform (etrain.info, sourced from the National Train Enquiry System), negative values are intentionally used to log early arrivals. A recorded delay of -15 physically means the train arrived 15 minutes ahead of its scheduled timetable. 
* Result: Negative values were retained intact. 
* Punctuality vs. Anomaly: While early arrivals are not the target of our outlier hunt, they are a primary concern for Punctuality & Temporal Pattern Analysis. Instead of being flagged as anomalies, these negative values will simply be aggregated into an "Early / On-Time" category when building the OLAP data cubes.

2. Noise Removal (Domain Knowledge Cutoff)
* Resolution Applied: In Indian Railways operations, a 24-hour (1,440-minute) delay is the standard operational cutoff. When a train is delayed beyond 24 hours, it is physically necessary to officially "reschedule" or cancel the journey to prevent conflicts with the next day's scheduled instance of that exact same train. 
* Result: Any continuous delay recorded above 1,440 minutes was classified as a transmission glitch, rollover error, or formally rescheduled train. A hard cutoff was applied to drop these records (15,564 rows), purging the noise without artificially flattening the extreme variance necessary for bottleneck analysis.

3. Outlier Boundary Definition (Statistical Dispersion)
* The Physics of Railway Speed (The Left Wall): Train delays are heavily right-skewed. A structural failure can easily delay a train by 15 hours, but a train physically cannot arrive 15 hours early due to mechanical speed limits and distance. Because the absolute maximum early arrival is exactly -120 minutes, there is no long "negative tail" hiding massive anomalies. The data hits a strict physical wall, meaning standard deviation is only a useful anomaly-detection tool on the boundless positive side.
* Defining a Bottleneck: A station where trains consistently arrive 90 minutes early is an indicator of high operational efficiency or padded timetables, not a network bottleneck. Therefore, we exclusively hunted for outliers on the positive side.
* Result: Global statistics were recalculated on the cleaned positive tail (True Mean: 35.6m, True SD: 66.9m). Two operational severity tiers were established:
  - The High-Risk Zone (2 Standard Deviations): Delays exceeding 169.46 minutes. This boundary identifies stations experiencing significant, systemic congestion, mapping broader geographical stress points.
  - The Critical Zone (3 Standard Deviations): Delays exceeding 236.37 minutes. This stricter boundary isolates the absolute worst structural failures, highlighting the truly "critical transit stations" that consistently trigger massive, cascading network delays.

4. Concept Hierarchy Generation (Time Dimension)

DATA WAREHOUSE DESIGN (STEP 3.1) — STAR SCHEMA CONSTRUCTION & ROLAP INTEGRATION

Following the successful completion of the ETL pipeline stage, the project advanced into next stage: Data Warehouse Design. The objective was to transform the clean, denormalized flat files into a multidimensional analytical space capable of supporting Online Analytical Processing (OLAP) and machine learning regression algorithms.

1. Central Fact Table (FACT_DELAY) Construction
* Technique Applied: Fact Table Generation with Degenerate Dimensions.
* Before: The numeric operational realities of a train's journey were split between two files: combined_delay_cleaned.csv (the delay facts) and combined_schedule_imputed.csv (the schedule facts and route distances).
* Resolution: Both datasets were merged into a centralized numeric core using train_no and station_no as the composite keys. station_no acts as a "degenerate dimension"—a dimensional sequence key that sits directly inside the fact table without needing a separate text-based descriptive table. Furthermore, the operational context rule discovered in Step 2.1 (Departure Delay at origin, Arrival Delay otherwise) was dynamically embedded into the schema.
* Result: A unified FACT_DELAY core containing both the scheduled expectations and the actual delay logs, locked at the precise grain of (date, train_no, station_no).

2. Starburst Dimensional Linking
* Technique Applied: Multidimensional Modeling (Star Schema).
* Resolution: The central FACT_DELAY table was radially connected to the three descriptive branches to enable categorical slicing:
    - Time Dimension: Linked via date to dim_date.date_id to establish temporal concept hierarchies (Quarter, Month, Weekend).
    - Train Dimension: Linked via train_no to dim_train_resolved.train_no to filter by vehicle category (e.g., Premium vs. Express).
    - Station Dimension: Linked via station_name to dim_station_resolved.station_name to enable geographic zoning.
* Result: The relational blueprint for OLAP data cubes was successfully established, proven by a successful roll-up aggregation test that grouped average delays by quarter and train type.

3. ROLAP View Implementation (Storage Optimization)
* Technique Applied: Relational OLAP (ROLAP) Virtualization.
* Resolution: Rather than exporting the fully joined multidimensional model into a massive, multi-gigabyte flat CSV (which would duplicate millions of text strings), the Star Schema was physically implemented as a dynamic VIEW inside a dedicated DuckDB database file.
* Result: The olap_star_schema view preserves a lightweight storage footprint while maintaining direct data lineage to the pristine Phase 1 CSVs in the results/ folder. It delivers millisecond querying performance for predictive modeling.
