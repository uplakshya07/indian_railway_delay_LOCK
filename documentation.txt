Problem Definition: Indian Railways generates massive volumes of operational data daily -- scheduled timetables, station master records, and actual arrival/departure logs. Train delays across extensive rail networks disrupt travel schedules, reduce operational efficiency, and cascade into system-wide congestion. This project aims to analyze historical train delay data to uncover underlying punctuality patterns, pinpoint high-risk stations and bottlenecks, and forecast delays to enable proactive operational and commuter planning.

Project Description: This project focuses on evaluating a comprehensive dataset of passenger train operations across the Indian Railways network over a full year. By analyzing scheduled timetables, station sequences, and recorded delays across multiple service types, the project addresses three primary objectives:
- Punctuality & Temporal Pattern Analysis: Characterizing delay distributions across different times of the year, days of the week, and train categories (e.g., Express, Superfast, Premium services).
- Bottleneck & High-Risk Route Identification: Mapping geographical and structural stress points across railway zones, identifying critical transit stations and sections that consistently trigger or amplify delays.
- Delay Prediction: Forecasting anticipated arrival and departure delays at subsequent stations along a route, transforming raw operational records into actionable foresight.

DATASET DETAILS:
Source: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/rxydenxd/indian-railways-delay-dataset

This dataset contains the delay information for about 7,000 trains from Indian railways network for the past one year (2025-02-08 to 2026-02-07), for each day, for each station in their route.

This dataset is scraped entirely from scratch using Python by the user with username "RXYDENXD" at www.kaggle.com. The goal was to build a comprehensive, multi-table dataset covering the schedules, station metadata, and real-world delay records for nearly the entire Indian Railways passenger network, spanning a full year of operations.

The scraping pipeline used requests, pandas, multi-threaded scraping, which reduced collection time by approximately 90% compared to sequential scraping.

What's Inside?
Ans: This dataset consists of four interlinked tables:

train_details.csv - 8,992 rows The master list of all trains in the dataset. Contains the train number, name, and service type for every train scraped. The 8,992 trains span 8 categories - Passenger, Express, Superfast, Rajdhani, Shatabdi, Vande Bharat, Garibrath, and Suvidha - covering effectively the full universe of publicly schedulable Indian Railways trains. (train_no,train_name,type_code)

station_full_names.csv - 8,963 rows Metadata for every unique station in the network. Includes the station code, full official name, railway zone, and address. Useful for geographic and zone-level aggregations. (station_name,station_full_name,station_zone,station_address)

combined_schedule.csv - 172,112 rows The complete timetable for all 8,992 trains. Each row represents one train's stop at one station, with scheduled arrival and departure times, arrival day (for multi-day journeys), station sequence number, and distance from the train's origin station. This table is the backbone for route-level analysis and journey duration calculations. (station_no,station_name,distance_from_origin,arrival_day,arrival_time,departure_day,departure_time,train_no)

combined_delay.csv - (~30 million) Millions of rows (~900MB) Contains 365 days of real-world delay records (in minutes) for trains at each station they stop at. This is the largest table by far and is what makes this dataset unique - a full year's worth of granular, station-level delay data across the Indian Railways network. (date,station_no,station_name,delay,train_no)

DATA UNDERSTANDING — DATA QUALITY AND RELATIONSHIP ANALYSIS

1. Duplicate Record Analysis
----------------------------

Complete duplicate records were checked across all four datasets.

Results:
- train_details.csv: 8,992 rows; 0 complete duplicate records detected.
- station_full_names.csv: 8,963 rows; 0 complete duplicate records detected.
- combined_schedule.csv: 172,112 rows; 0 complete duplicate records detected.
- combined_delay.csv: 38,428,703 rows; no complete duplicates were detected during the chunk-wise scan.

Note:
The duplicate analysis for combined_delay.csv was performed in chunks because of its large size. Therefore, the result does not by itself prove that duplicate rows do not exist across chunk boundaries. A global duplicate verification should be performed during the later key-resolution stage.

Conclusion:
No complete duplicate records were detected in the smaller datasets. The delay dataset also showed no duplicates in the chunk-wise scan, but a final global verification is required before the warehouse is finalized.


2. Missing-Value Analysis
-------------------------

Missing values were analyzed for all datasets.

train_details.csv:
- No missing values were detected.

station_full_names.csv:
- station_address: 94 missing values.
- station_name: no missing values.
- Other analyzed attributes: no missing values.

combined_schedule.csv:
- arrival_time: 8,673 missing values, approximately 5.04%.
- departure_time: 8,673 missing values, approximately 5.04%.
- Other analyzed attributes: no missing values.

combined_delay.csv:
- delay: 1,874,271 missing values out of 38,428,703 records, approximately 4.88%.
- Non-missing delay values: 36,554,432.

Important Observation:
The missing delay values are not uniformly distributed. Some stations have extremely high or complete missingness. Examples include:
- BNDE: 104/104 missing.
- BSCN: 52/52 missing.
- HHAL: 261/261 missing.
- AJLE: 365/365 missing.
- STLA: 365/365 missing.
- MLYC: 728/730 missing.
- KCVL: 9,107/9,281 missing.

Date-level analysis also showed periods of increased missingness, particularly around February 2025. For example:
- 2025-02-25: 14,227/104,672 missing (13.59%).
- 2025-02-24: 13,926/105,151 missing (13.24%).
- 2025-02-26: 13,648/104,458 missing (13.07%).
- 2025-02-18: 13,049/104,688 missing (12.46%).
- 2025-02-19: 12,977/104,513 missing (12.42%).

Conclusion:
Missing delay values should not be automatically replaced with zero. The missingness appears to have a structured pattern and should be investigated during ETL and key-resolution stages before deciding how these records will be represented in the data warehouse.


3. Key Uniqueness Analysis
--------------------------

train_details.csv contains:
- 8,992 total rows.
- 8,720 unique train_no values.

Therefore, train_no is not unique in the raw train master.

Further investigation showed that some duplicate train numbers are associated with different type_code values while often retaining the same train name. Examples include:
- 1042: SNSI DR SPECIAL EXP-TRAINS / SNSI DR SPECIAL PRM-TRAINS.
- 1265: MML ABKP SPL PRM-TRAINS / MML ABKP SPL EXP-TRAINS.
- 2003: NDLS SWARNA SHATABDI SHT-TRAINS / NDLS SWARNA SHATABDI PRM-TRAINS.

Grouping duplicate train_no values showed cases where:
- row_count = 2
- train_names = 1
- type_codes = 2

Conclusion:
train_no alone should not yet be assumed to be the primary key of the train dimension. The relationship between train_no, type_code, train_name, schedule records, and delay records must be investigated before selecting the final warehouse key.


4. Schedule Structure Analysis
------------------------------

combined_schedule.csv contains:
- 172,112 rows.
- 119 unique station_no values.
- 8,629 unique station_name values.

Interpretation:
station_no does not represent a globally unique physical station. Instead, it represents the ordinal position of a station within a train's route or journey.

station_name represents the physical station.

Conclusion:
The warehouse should not use station_no as the station dimension key. The distinction between route-stop sequence and physical station identity must be preserved in the warehouse design.


5. Referential Integrity Analysis
---------------------------------

Train Master → Schedule:
- 8,673 unique train numbers are present in combined_schedule.csv.
- 0 schedule train numbers were unmatched in train_details.csv.
- 47 train numbers present in train_details.csv do not appear in combined_schedule.csv.

Train Master → Delay:
- 7,033 unique train numbers are present in combined_delay.csv.
- 0 delay train numbers were unmatched in train_details.csv.
- 1,687 train numbers present in train_details.csv do not appear in combined_delay.csv.

Station Master → Schedule:
- 1 schedule station was not found in station_full_names.csv.
- 335 stations in station_full_names.csv do not appear in combined_schedule.csv.

Station Master → Delay:
- 63 delay stations were not found in station_full_names.csv.
- 799 stations in station_full_names.csv do not appear in combined_delay.csv.

Conclusion:
Most foreign-key relationships are consistent, but the unmatched station values require investigation. Possible explanations include naming or formatting differences, renamed stations, missing master records, or genuine data-quality issues. These records should not be deleted automatically.


6. Delay Fact Grain Analysis
----------------------------

A candidate natural key was defined as:

(date, train_no, station_no)

This represents one observed delay for:
- one train,
- on one date,
- at one route-stop position.

The candidate combination was checked for duplicate occurrences within the processed chunks, and no duplicate combinations were detected during the preliminary chunk-wise analysis.

However, because combined_delay.csv contains 38.4 million records, global uniqueness across all records still needs to be verified.

Conclusion:
(date, train_no, station_no) is a strong candidate for the natural grain of the delay fact table, but it should not be finalized until global uniqueness has been verified.


7. Overall Findings
-------------------

The Data Quality and Relationship Analysis established the following:

1. No complete duplicates were detected in train_details.csv, station_full_names.csv, or combined_schedule.csv.
2. No complete duplicates were detected in the chunk-wise scan of combined_delay.csv, but global verification is still required.
3. Missing values exist in station_address, arrival_time, departure_time, and delay.
4. Delay missingness is structured by station and date rather than being uniformly distributed.
5. train_no is not unique in the train master and therefore cannot yet be treated as the final train dimension key.
6. station_no represents route-stop order rather than physical station identity.
7. Referential integrity is generally strong, but unmatched station values require investigation.
8. (date, train_no, station_no) is a strong candidate for the delay fact-table grain, pending global uniqueness verification.


8. Conclusion
-------------

The datasets are sufficiently structured to proceed toward ETL and data warehouse design, but several key issues must be resolved before finalizing the warehouse schema.

The most important unresolved issues are:
- resolving duplicate train_no values,
- investigating unmatched schedule and delay stations,
- globally verifying the uniqueness of (date, train_no, station_no),
- determining the final keys for train and station dimensions, and
- deciding how structured missing delay values will be represented during ETL.

These issues should be handled in a separate Key Resolution stage rather than being silently corrected during the initial data-understanding phase.
