Problem Definition: Indian Railways generates massive volumes of operational data daily -- scheduled timetables, station master records, and actual arrival/departure logs. Train delays across extensive rail networks disrupt travel schedules, reduce operational efficiency, and cascade into system-wide congestion. This project aims to analyze historical train delay data to uncover underlying punctuality patterns, pinpoint high-risk stations and bottlenecks, and forecast delays to enable proactive operational and commuter planning.

Project Description: This project focuses on evaluating a comprehensive dataset of passenger train operations across the Indian Railways network over a full year. By analyzing scheduled timetables, station sequences, and recorded delays across multiple service types, the project addresses three primary objectives:
- Punctuality & Temporal Pattern Analysis: Characterizing delay distributions across different times of the year, days of the week, and train categories (e.g., Express, Superfast, Premium services).
- Bottleneck & High-Risk Route Identification: Mapping geographical and structural stress points across railway zones, identifying critical transit stations and sections that consistently trigger or amplify delays.
- Delay Prediction: Forecasting anticipated arrival and departure delays at subsequent stations along a route, transforming raw operational records into actionable foresight.

DATASET DETAILS:
Source: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/rxydenxd/indian-railways-delay-dataset

This dataset contains the delay information for about 7,000 trains from Indian railways network for the past one year (2025-02-08 to 2026-02-07), for each day, for each station in their route.

This dataset is scraped entirely from scratch using Python by the user with username "RXYDENXD" at www.kaggle.com. The goal was to build a comprehensive, multi-table dataset covering the schedules, station metadata, and real-world delay records for nearly the entire Indian Railways passenger network, spanning a full year of operations.

The scraping pipeline used requests, pandas, multi-threaded scraping, which reduced collection time by approximately 90% compared to sequential scraping.

What's Inside?
Ans: This dataset consists of four interlinked tables:

train_details.csv - 8,992 rows The master list of all trains in the dataset. Contains the train number, name, and service type for every train scraped. The 8,992 trains span 8 categories - Passenger, Express, Superfast, Rajdhani, Shatabdi, Vande Bharat, Garibrath, and Suvidha - covering effectively the full universe of publicly schedulable Indian Railways trains. (train_no,train_name,type_code)

station_full_names.csv - 8,963 rows Metadata for every unique station in the network. Includes the station code, full official name, railway zone, and address. Useful for geographic and zone-level aggregations. (station_name,station_full_name,station_zone,station_address)

combined_schedule.csv - 172,112 rows The complete timetable for all 8,992 trains. Each row represents one train's stop at one station, with scheduled arrival and departure times, arrival day (for multi-day journeys), station sequence number, and distance from the train's origin station. This table is the backbone for route-level analysis and journey duration calculations. (station_no,station_name,distance_from_origin,arrival_day,arrival_time,departure_day,departure_time,train_no)

combined_delay.csv - (~30 million) Millions of rows (~900MB) Contains 365 days of real-world delay records (in minutes) for trains at each station they stop at. This is the largest table by far and is what makes this dataset unique - a full year's worth of granular, station-level delay data across the Indian Railways network. (date,station_no,station_name,delay,train_no)

The combined_delay.csv file provides a single column named delay, which the author simply defines as the delay (in minutes) that a train experienced at a specific station stop.

Note: In standard Indian Railways tracking systems (like NTES, which is typically used for this kind of scraping), "delay" generally refers to the arrival delay at intermediate stations and the departure delay at the train's originating station. We will resolve it during ETL and cleaning stage.

DATA UNDERSTANDING — DATA QUALITY AND RELATIONSHIP ANALYSIS

1. Duplicate Record Analysis
----------------------------

Complete duplicate records were checked across all four datasets.

Results:

Conclusion:
No complete duplicate records were detected in the smaller datasets. The delay dataset also showed no duplicates in the chunk-wise scan, but a final global verification is required before the warehouse is finalized.

3. Key Uniqueness Analysis
--------------------------

train_details.csv contains:
- 8,992 total rows.
- 8,720 unique train_no values.

Therefore, train_no is not unique in the raw train master.

Further investigation showed that some duplicate train numbers are associated with different type_code values while often retaining the same train name. Examples include:
- 1042: SNSI DR SPECIAL EXP-TRAINS / SNSI DR SPECIAL PRM-TRAINS.
- 1265: MML ABKP SPL PRM-TRAINS / MML ABKP SPL EXP-TRAINS.
- 2003: NDLS SWARNA SHATABDI SHT-TRAINS / NDLS SWARNA SHATABDI PRM-TRAINS.

Grouping duplicate train_no values showed cases where:
- row_count = 2
- train_names = 1
- type_codes = 2

Conclusion:
train_no alone should not yet be assumed to be the primary key of the train dimension. The relationship between train_no, type_code, train_name, schedule records, and delay records must be investigated before selecting the final warehouse key.


4. Schedule Structure Analysis
------------------------------

combined_schedule.csv contains:
- 172,112 rows.
- 119 unique station_no values.
- 8,629 unique station_name values.

Interpretation:
station_no does not represent a globally unique physical station. Instead, it represents the ordinal position of a station within a train's route or journey.

station_name represents the physical station.

Conclusion:
The warehouse should not use station_no as the station dimension key. The distinction between route-stop sequence and physical station identity must be preserved in the warehouse design.

5 KEY RESOLUTIONS (STEP 1.3) — RESOLVING STRUCTURAL AMBIGUITIES

Following the Data Quality and Relationship Analysis, a dedicated Key Resolution stage was executed using DuckDB's out-of-core processing capabilities to handle the massive datasets efficiently without chunk-boundary issues.

1. Resolve Duplicate train_no

Initial Discovery: 272 duplicate train_no values were detected. Analysis revealed that these duplicates often shared the same train_name but possessed different type_code values (e.g., a base service type coupled with an overlay tag like 'PRM-TRAINS').

Resolution Rule Applied: The base service type (e.g., EXP, SF, SHT) was retained as the primary_type_code, while the PRM-TRAINS designation was converted into a boolean flag (is_premium_special).

Manual Review Completed: Four specific "NAMO BHARAT RAPID RAIL" rows required manual intervention. These were manually updated via SQL to the T18-TRAINS primary type code, successfully finalizing the 8,720-row train dimension (dim_train_resolved) without dropping valid records.

2. Investigate Unmatched Stations

Initial Discovery: Referential integrity checks flagged 1 schedule station and 63 delay stations that did not exist in the master station_full_names.csv file.

Resolution Applied: Rather than dropping these operational records or breaking the OLAP architecture, these missing station codes were isolated via NOT IN queries and appended to the final station dimension (dim_station_resolved). Placeholder metadata values ("UNKNOWN") were assigned for the full name, zone, and address fields to maintain referential integrity without discarding valuable data.

3. Globally Verify Natural-Key Uniqueness

Initial Discovery: The preliminary chunk-wise scan of the ~30 million row delay dataset could not definitively rule out duplicates across chunk boundaries.

Resolution Applied: Utilizing DuckDB's dynamic memory management, an out-of-core global grouping query evaluated all 38,428,703 rows in a single pass to test the combination of (date, train_no, station_no) for duplicate instances.

4. Determine Final Fact-Table Grain

ETL / DATA CLEANING (STEP 2.1) — MISSING VALUE IMPUTATION AND FIELD RESOLUTION

Following the Key Resolution stage, the ETL and Data Cleaning phase commenced, focusing on missing value imputation and field verification using Pandas and DuckDB out-of-core processing. To preserve data integrity, the original raw datasets in the `data_sets/` directory remained completely unmodified. All cleaning scripts were executed via Jupyter notebooks stored in the `analysis/` directory, and the transformed outputs were routed exclusively to the `results/` directory.

1. Dimension Table Cleaning: Missing Station Addresses
------------------------------------------------------
*   Technique Used: Application of a Global Constant for Nominal Attributes.
*   Before: The resolved station dimension (`dim_station_resolved.csv`) contained 94 missing `station_address` values.
*   Resolution Applied: A Pandas script replaced these null fields with the explicit global constant "UNKNOWN".
*   After/Result: 0 remaining null addresses. Referential integrity is strictly maintained for the OLAP warehouse without discarding any dimensional records. The output was saved as `results/dim_station_resolved.csv`.

2. Schedule Fact Cleaning: Missing Arrival & Departure Times
------------------------------------------------------------
*   Technique Used: Logical Imputation (Zero-Halt Rule) for Interval-Scaled Attributes.
*   Before: The `combined_schedule.csv` dataset had 8,673 missing `arrival_time` and 8,673 missing `departure_time` values. A verification script proved these missing states were structural and intentional: missing arrivals occurred exactly and only at origin stations (`station_no = 1`), while missing departures occurred exactly and only at destination stations.
*   Resolution Applied: The Zero-Halt imputation rule was applied using Pandas. Missing origin arrival times were filled with their scheduled departure time, and missing destination departure times were filled with their scheduled arrival time.
*   After/Result: 0 remaining missing schedule times. This resolves the missing data while preserving the continuous numeric sequences required for time-series predictive models. The cleaned dataset was saved as `results/combined_schedule_imputed.csv`.

3. Delay Field Context Resolution (important for handling missing delay values)
---------------------------------
*   Technique Used: Contextual Hypothesis Testing via Out-of-Core Processing.
*   Before: The exact operational definition of the `delay` column in the ~30 million row `combined_delay.csv` dataset was ambiguous (whether it represented arrival delay or departure delay).
    Our defined composite hypothesis posits that the delay field is a context-dependent metric that switches meaning based on the station's position in the route. Specifically, it assumes that:
        - Departure Delay is recorded exclusively at the train's source or origin station.
        - Arrival Delay is recorded at all subsequent intermediate and destination stations.

*   Resolution Applied: A hypothesis was formulated to test delays at route boundaries (origin vs. destination). A DuckDB SQL query, explicitly filtering out null values, evaluated all 38 million records and yielded 1,038,047 non-zero delays at origin stations and 1,044,006 non-zero delays at destination stations.
*   After/Result: The composite context switch hypothesis was confirmed. The `delay` field is context-dependent: it strictly records Departure Delay at the source station (`station_no = 1`) and Arrival Delay at all intermediate and destination stations (`station_no > 1`). These findings establish the foundational ETL rules for numeric modeling and were successfully exported to `results/step02_01_ETL_FINDINGS.json`.
