Problem Definition: Indian Railways generates massive volumes of operational data daily -- scheduled timetables, station master records, and actual arrival/departure logs. Train delays across extensive rail networks disrupt travel schedules, reduce operational efficiency, and cascade into system-wide congestion. This project aims to analyze historical train delay data to uncover underlying punctuality patterns, pinpoint high-risk stations and bottlenecks, and forecast delays to enable proactive operational and commuter planning.

Project Description: This project focuses on evaluating a comprehensive dataset of passenger train operations across the Indian Railways network over a full year. By analyzing scheduled timetables, station sequences, and recorded delays across multiple service types, the project addresses three primary objectives:
- Punctuality & Temporal Pattern Analysis: Characterizing delay distributions across different times of the year, days of the week, and train categories (e.g., Express, Superfast, Premium services).
- Bottleneck & High-Risk Route Identification: Mapping geographical and structural stress points across railway zones, identifying critical transit stations and sections that consistently trigger or amplify delays.
- Delay Prediction: Forecasting anticipated arrival and departure delays at subsequent stations along a route, transforming raw operational records into actionable foresight.

DATASET DETAILS:
Source: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/rxydenxd/indian-railways-delay-dataset

This dataset contains the delay information for about 7,000 trains from Indian railways network for the past one year (2025-02-08 to 2026-02-07), for each day, for each station in their route.

This dataset is scraped entirely from scratch using Python by the user with username "RXYDENXD" at www.kaggle.com. The goal was to build a comprehensive, multi-table dataset covering the schedules, station metadata, and real-world delay records for nearly the entire Indian Railways passenger network, spanning a full year of operations.

The scraping pipeline used requests, pandas, multi-threaded scraping, which reduced collection time by approximately 90% compared to sequential scraping.

What's Inside?
Ans: This dataset consists of four interlinked tables:

train_details.csv - 8,992 rows The master list of all trains in the dataset. Contains the train number, name, and service type for every train scraped. The 8,992 trains span 8 categories - Passenger, Express, Superfast, Rajdhani, Shatabdi, Vande Bharat, Garibrath, and Suvidha - covering effectively the full universe of publicly schedulable Indian Railways trains. (train_no,train_name,type_code)

station_full_names.csv - 8,963 rows Metadata for every unique station in the network. Includes the station code, full official name, railway zone, and address. Useful for geographic and zone-level aggregations. (station_name,station_full_name,station_zone,station_address)

combined_schedule.csv - 172,112 rows The complete timetable for all 8,992 trains. Each row represents one train's stop at one station, with scheduled arrival and departure times, arrival day (for multi-day journeys), station sequence number, and distance from the train's origin station. This table is the backbone for route-level analysis and journey duration calculations. (station_no,station_name,distance_from_origin,arrival_day,arrival_time,departure_day,departure_time,train_no)

combined_delay.csv - (~30 million) Millions of rows (~900MB) Contains 365 days of real-world delay records (in minutes) for trains at each station they stop at. This is the largest table by far and is what makes this dataset unique - a full year's worth of granular, station-level delay data across the Indian Railways network. (date,station_no,station_name,delay,train_no)

ATTRIBUTE TYPES:
Dataset: train_details.csv
    train_no: Nominal. Although represented by numbers, ID numbers act as names or categories and do not have measurable magnitude.  
    train_name: String / Nominal. Textual representations of categories or names of things. (e.g. DURG ABKP EXP)
    type_code: Nominal. Represents distinct categorical states (e.g., Passenger, Express, Rajdhani).   
Dataset: station_full_names.csv   
    station_name & station_full_name: String / Nominal. Textual categories used to identify entities.   
    station_zone: Nominal. Represents a specific categorical region.   
    station_address: String. A textual attribute consisting of character sequences.   
combined_schedule.csv   
    station_no: Ordinal. This acts as a station sequence number, meaning its values have a meaningful order and ranking (e.g., 1st stop, 2nd stop).
    distance_from_origin: Numeric (Ratio-scaled). A quantitative measurement with an inherent zero-point, allowing you to say one distance is a specific magnitude larger than another.   
    arrival_day & departure_day: Ordinal or Numeric (Interval-scaled). Days possess a meaningful order and can be measured in equal-sized units.
    arrival_time & departure_time: Date or Numeric (Interval-scaled). Times can be formatted as specialized date/time strings or treated mathematically as interval-scaled units.
    train_no & station_name: Nominal and String / Nominal, respectively.
combined_delay.csv   
    date: Date. Represents a specific calendar date, which can be stored as a formatted string or converted internally to numeric form.   
    delay: Numeric (Ratio-scaled). A measurable quantitative value recorded in minutes, possessing a true zero-point (zero minutes of delay).   train_no, station_no, & station_name: Retain their Nominal, Ordinal, and String / Nominal mappings from the previous tables.
